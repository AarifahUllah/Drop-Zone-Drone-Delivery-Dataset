Fly a mission, help train safer delivery drones

Fly two or three short drone missions over a house, a park, or a rooftop, then upload the drone video, flight log and a short description. The data helps researchers train perception and reasoning AI models that make drone delivery more intelligent and safe.

About the data collection effort

What you record

2–3 short videos of a slow, vertical descent, camera pointing straight down.

What you upload

For each video: the video, its flight log (e.g. DJI .MP4 + .SRT) and a one-paragraph description (.txt).

Time needed

About 2 minutes of flying per video, plus setup.

Access the data

Sample videos, flight logs and descriptions are on Hugging Face. Approved contributions are added there.

Dataset on Hugging Face

How to fly a data collection flight

Pretend you're a delivery drone bringing a package to a house, park, townhouse or rooftop.

  1. Start: 100–150 ft (30–45 m), hover 10–30 s Fly almost directly above your delivery spot. Point the camera straight down and start recording.
  2. Descend slowly to 25–30 ft (about 8–9 m) Gentle throttle, smooth and slow. Keep recording.
  3. Deliver: hover 30–50 s Hold steady while you imagine lowering a package on a rope and reeling it back up.
  4. Stop recording. Done! The climb up doesn't need to be recorded.

Make each scene different

Change at least one thing between your 2–3 videos:

Lighting

Dawn, morning, noon, dusk; sunny or overcast

Delivery spot

Driveway, yard, rooftop, park

Nearby objects

Cars, bikes, trees, pets, people (with consent)

Write a short description

For each video, save one paragraph in a text file with the same name as the video (e.g. DJI_0042.txt; Notepad works). Include:

  • Date, time of day and weather
  • Type of delivery spot (driveway, yard, rooftop…)
  • Objects near the spot
  • Whether anything enters or blocks the spot, and when
DJI_0042.txt Recorded Oct 12 at about 5:30 pm, overcast with light wind. The delivery spot is the paved driveway of a suburban house. A silver car is parked to the left and a large tree overhangs the top of the frame. A dog walks across the spot around 0:45, briefly blocking it, then leaves.

Safety tips

  • Follow local rules. US: check FAA B4UFLY (DC has a 15-nautical-mile no-drone zone). Elsewhere: your aviation authority.
  • Good weather only. Most drones can't handle rain or mist.
  • Full battery. Hovering drains it quickly.
  • DJI: turn on Camera settings → Video subtitles to get the .SRT log.
  • Slow and steady. Gentle throttle; any pitch or roll gradually.
  • Camera straight down (gimbal at −90°).
  • Record continuously from the top hover to the end of the delivery hover.
  • Keep the drone in sight, and watch your own footing and traffic.

Privacy

Flight logs contain GPS coordinates. We remove exact locations before anything is published.
  • Only film people who have agreed. Avoid neighbors' private spaces.
  • Your name and email are used only to contact and credit you. They are never published.
  • Contributions are released under CC BY 4.0.
  • To withdraw a submission, email aarifahullah@gwu.edu.
