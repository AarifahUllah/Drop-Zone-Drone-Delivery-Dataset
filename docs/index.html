About the project

Autonomous delivery drones need to recognize a safe drop spot and notice what's around it: people, pets, cars, trees. To train and test these systems, researchers need real descent footage from many places, in many conditions. This project collects that footage from volunteer pilots worldwide and releases it as an open research dataset.

What you record

2–3 short videos of a slow, vertical descent with the camera pointing straight down.

What you upload

Each video file plus its matching flight log, for example DJI .MP4 + .SRT.

Time needed

About 2 minutes of flying per video, plus setup.

See the data

Sample flights, with their full-length videos and flight logs, are available to browse and download on Hugging Face. Every contributed flight that passes review is added there.

How to fly a collection flight

Pretend you're a delivery drone bringing a package to a house, park, townhouse, or apartment rooftop.

  1. Start position: 100–150 ft (30–45 m), hover 10–30 s Fly to a point almost directly above your landing spot (driveway, front yard, backyard…). Point the camera straight down and start recording, then hold a steady hover.
  2. Descend slowly to 25–30 ft (about 8–9 m) Lower the drone smoothly and slowly with gentle throttle. Keep recording continuously.
  3. Delivery position: hover 30–50 s Hold steady while you imagine lowering a package on a rope and reeling it back up.
  4. Stop recording. Done! You've captured one delivery. The climb up doesn't need to be recorded.

Make each scene different

Please record 2–3 videos around your location, changing something each time. Variety is what makes the dataset useful.

Lighting

Dawn, morning, noon, afternoon, dusk; sunny or overcast.

Landing spot

Driveway, front yard, backyard, rooftop, or wherever you'd want a delivery.

Nearby objects

Cars, bikes, trees, furniture, pets, or people (only people who have agreed to be filmed).

Tips for a safe, successful flight

Follow your local drone rules

Rules differ by country. In the US, check airspace with the FAA's B4UFLY; airports and government sites are restricted, and Washington, DC has a 15-nautical-mile no-drone zone. Elsewhere, check your national aviation authority. Never fly where it's prohibited.

Before you fly

  • Check the weather. Clear or cloudy days are best. Most drones can't handle rain or mist.
  • Charge fully. Hovering drains batteries quickly; a full charge lets you capture the whole sequence without stopping.
  • Commercial drones (e.g. DJI) are preferred because they hold a stable hover, but any drone you fly confidently is welcome.
  • Read your drone's manual and follow its recommended practices.

While you fly

  • Slow and steady. Use gentle throttle to descend. Adjust yaw as needed; if you need pitch or roll, apply it gradually.
  • Camera straight down. If your gimbal tilts, set it to −90°.
  • Record continuously from the start hover through the end of the delivery hover, with no breaks.
  • Keep the drone in sight and watch for birds and branches. Watch your own footing too: stay away from traffic and tripping hazards.

DJI users: turn on the flight log

In DJI Fly or DJI GO, enable Camera settings → Video subtitles (on some models: Video Caption). The drone will then save a .SRT file next to each .MP4, with the same name.

Privacy and consent

Your flight log contains GPS coordinates. DJI .SRT files record latitude and longitude, which can reveal where you live. We remove exact coordinates before any public release and keep the originals private to the research team.
  • Only film people who have agreed to it. Avoid neighbors' windows and private yards.
  • Your name and email are used only to contact you about your submission and to credit you (if you choose). They are never published in the dataset.
  • Contributed footage is released under CC BY 4.0 for research and other uses.
  • To withdraw a submission, email aarifahullah@example.edu.
